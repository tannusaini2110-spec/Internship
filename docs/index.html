POS. 01 RESEARCH PAPER — CONTENT DECLINE DETECTION

Which pages are already losing search visibility — and which are worth fixing first?

A client-grouped, leakage-audited model for flagging declining content, benchmarked against a transparent baseline on 176,738 pages of real search-performance data — with a ranked, reason-coded review queue as the output.

Author — Tannu Saini Track — FlyRank ML Internship Repo — tannusaini2110-spec/Internship Date — 2026
POS. 01

Abstract

Question. Which pages in a content portfolio are already losing search performance, and which of those are worth a person's time to review first?

Data. 176,738 pages across 47 pseudonymous clients, drawn from the FlyRank ML Internship warehouse's March 2026 search-performance snapshot (Google Search Console and GA4 fields).

Method. A Random Forest classifier trained on five features that are not derived from the label, validated with a client-grouped holdout (zero client overlap between train and test) and audited for leakage, compared against a transparent ranking-rule baseline on the identical split.

Result. On 12 held-out clients never seen in training, the model reached F1 0.872 versus 0.129 for the baseline rule on the same test set. A naive random-row split would have shown 0.845 accuracy versus the honest client-grouped 0.832 — a real but modest 1.3-point gap, confirming the result is not simply an artifact of split selection.

Use. The model's output feeds a ranked, reason-coded review queue — decision-support for the kind of portfolio-scale triage problem FlyRank's own research quantified (703.2K cannibalizing queries, 73.2M impressions at risk across 341,701 pieces), not an autonomous action system.

POS. 02

Introduction

A content team with dozens of clients cannot manually re-check every page every week. Someone still has to decide what to look at first.

That's the decision this work supports: given a portfolio of pages and their recent search behavior, produce a short, explainable list of pages worth a person's attention this week — not a verdict, a triage aid. The cost of getting this wrong in either direction is real: missing a genuinely declining page means it keeps losing ground unnoticed, while flooding an editor with false alarms trains them to ignore the list entirely.

The lane here is content decline detection: using position, impression, and engagement signals already sitting in Search Console and GA4 exports to flag pages that look like they're heading the wrong way, before someone has to notice it by hand.

The case study behind this lane FlyRank's own March 2026 portfolio research (341,701 content pieces across 57 brands) put a number on exactly this bottleneck: 703.2K cannibalizing queries and 73.2M impressions sitting at risk portfolio-wide, two-thirds of those cases rated "Critical" severity, and 41.8% of the portfolio never indexed at all. At that scale, no editor is opening every page by hand — a systematic, explainable triage layer stops being a nice-to-have and becomes the only way review time gets spent on the right pages. That gap between portfolio size and human review capacity is the problem this model targets.

POS. 04

Methodology

Label definition

is_declining = 1 if a page's total March clicks fall at or below the 30th percentile of clicks across the portfolio, else 0.

Honest note A large share of pages in this portfolio have zero clicks, so many pages tie exactly at the 30th-percentile threshold. The result: the actual positive rate is 61.1%, not a clean 30%. This is disclosed here rather than smoothed over — it's a real property of this label, not a bug hidden from the reader.

Features

Five fields, none derived from the label: avg_position, total_impressions, avg_engaged_sessions, total_pageviews, days_seen.

Baseline

A transparent rule, not a model: rank pages by 0.6 × (inverse average-position rank) + 0.4 × (days-seen rank), flag the top 30%.

Validation design

Split by client_hash_id using a grouped shuffle split (25% held out) — chosen because pages from the same client share business and domain traits; a row-level random split would let client-specific patterns leak between train and test. Result: 133,474 training pages across 35 clients, 43,264 test pages across 12 clients, zero client overlap confirmed directly.

Leakage checks

Two checks were run before trusting the split:

Validation audit
Naive random-row split84.5%
Honest client-grouped split83.2%
total_clicks included (leaky) — excluded from final model100.0%
The naive split overstated accuracy by 1.3 points — small, but real, and the honest number is what's reported everywhere else in this paper. Including total_clicks pushes accuracy to 100% because it directly defines the label; this confirmed it had to be excluded as a feature.

POS. 06

Limitations

What this work cannot claim, stated plainly rather than left for a reader to discover.

  • One month, cross-sectional. This is a March 2026 snapshot — there is no multi-month trend confirming that flagged pages actually kept declining afterward.
  • The label is a proxy, not a definition. Bottom-30th-percentile clicks is a stand-in for "declining," and the 61.1% actual positive rate (noted in Methodology) shows the proxy is noisier than the name suggests. A page can land here by being small-audience-by-design, not by losing ground.
  • Twelve held-out clients. A modest sample for judging how well this generalizes to entirely new clients or business types.
  • No content-type, seasonality, or query-intent signal. These aren't in the current feature set at all.
  • Association, not cause. The model says a page's profile resembles historically low-click pages — it does not say why, and it does not support "doing X will fix Y" language anywhere in this paper.
  • Portfolio-specific. Nothing here is a claim about how search engines rank content in general — only an observed pattern in this dataset, this month.

What should NOT be automated

No-go list
  • No automatic publishing, unpublishing, redirecting, or deleting content from this score.
  • No automatic client-facing reporting generated directly off decline_probability.
  • This queue does not replace FlyRank's existing human-reviewed optimization flags — it's a prioritization input alongside them.
  • No action taken on a single page from this model alone, without a person opening the page first.
  • MODEL_FLAGGED_OTHER and borderline-score pages (0.45–0.55) always get a manual second look before anything is scheduled.

Monitoring & retrain triggers

  • Re-score against each new monthly partition as it lands.
  • Base-rate drift — if the declining-page share moves materially from ~61%, the label threshold itself needs re-examination.
  • Accuracy drop — if honest, client-grouped accuracy on a new month falls more than 5 points below this baseline, treat the model as stale.
  • Feature drift on total_impressions — the dominant driver by a wide margin; a shift in its distribution is the most likely thing to quietly break these predictions.
  • Scheduled retrain — monthly regardless of the above, since the warehouse adds a new month every cycle and this model has only ever seen March 2026.
